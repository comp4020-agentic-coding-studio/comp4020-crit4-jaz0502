1. What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?  
The breakthrough that moved the work forward was thinking about how I could make the instrument interactive without giving the user obvious instructions. Initially, I thought about creating a wind chime interaction where the user would have to “touch” the wind chimes to make them move. However, I then thought about how real wind chimes work when they are hanging outside in a garden. Wind chimes are moved by the wind rather than being directly touched by a person. This led me to change the interaction so that the user controls the wind instead, creating movement and sound indirectly through their actions. This made the interaction feel more natural and playful, while still allowing the user to discover how the instrument works for themselves.

2. What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?  
This work made me more interested in exploring sound as a way of interacting with software, rather than relying on visuals. I found it interesting that sound could be an active part of the interaction, where the user's actions directly shape what they hear. As a software developer, I want to explore more creative forms of interaction and think beyond conventional interfaces. I want to create experiences that encourage people to experiment, rather than always telling them exactly what to do.
