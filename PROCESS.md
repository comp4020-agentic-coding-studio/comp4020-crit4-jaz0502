# Process overview

## What I built

I built an interactive wind chime garden where the user acts as the wind. The user moves their mouse around the garden, which affects the wind chimes and causes them to move and produce different sounds depending on the direction and strength of the wind. The interaction is designed to be intuitive and exploratory, with no wrong way to play. The user can freely move their mouse around and discover how different movements affect the visual and audio response. The interactives also work with the keyboard (using arrow keys), and by touchscreen.

## The moments that mattered

Moment - Redesigning the overall aesthetics:

The first prototype Claude created was very simplistic, using a blank background and black lines to represent the wind chimes. The result was visually unclear, as it was not immediately obvious that the lines represented physical wind chimes. Instead of continuing to refine the abstract representation, I decided to ground the instrument in a garden setting, making the environment part of the experience and giving the chimes a more recognisable context. Claude's next prototype introduced a garden, but it was visually dull and did not capture the brighter, slightly magical but still realistic atmosphere I wanted. Rather than simply asking Claude to “make it better”, I gave it more specific visual direction based on the intended aesthetic, including the lighting, colour, and atmosphere. I knew this approach was working when the revised prototype made the wind chimes immediately recognisable while integrating them naturally into the garden, and the overall scene was much closer to the intended atmosphere.
